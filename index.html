01 — Current cart experience

Each change sends the customer through the same flow: leave the cart, find an alternative, then return to check the total.

Method

Current-state task flow

Audience

A price-sensitive shopper at a discount grocery retailer, balancing price and value

What the flow shows

The customer can make each change, but only by leaving the cart and coming back.

I treated that repeated effort as the core problem in the current experience, especially this close to checkout.

02 — Existing patterns

Existing cart patterns already help customers resolve specific item changes without returning to search.

Direct precedent

Zepto · Swap & Save

What it proves
A lower-priced alternative can appear beside an item already in the cart, so the customer can make the change without starting a new search.
Scope
The pattern is tied to one specific item and one clear reason: paying less.

Supporting precedent

Instacart · Item Substitution

What it proves
A replacement can be shown with a clear reason, such as similarity or value, while the final choice stays with the customer.
Scope
The pattern is tied to an item that needs a replacement because of availability.

What I took from these patterns

  • Keep the change in the cart

    The customer should not need to return to search.

  • Explain why an option is shown

    Price, value, similarity, or another clear reason.

  • Keep the customer in control

    The product surfaces the option; the customer decides.

The limitation: both patterns solve a specific item-level need. Neither starts from the customer wanting to spend less or get more value across the cart.

03 — The reframe

Instead of sending customers back to search, the cart can show relevant alternatives and what each one would do to the total.

Today, customers leave the cart to find an alternative, then return to check the result.

I wanted those two parts of the decision to stay together.

Show the option

A relevant alternative appears without sending the customer back to search.

Explain the reason

The customer can see why the option is shown and how it differs from the current item.

Preview the result

The new cart total is visible before anything changes.

Customer control

The product shows the options and their impact. The customer decides what changes.

How might we

Help customers spend less or get more value after seeing the cart total, without sending them back through shopping item by item?

04 — Product direction

I started with Basket Optimizer where the friction appears: after the customer sees the total.

If it proves useful, customers could save that basket and start from it next time.

Current shop

  1. Build cart
  2. See total
  3. Checkout

Basket Optimizer

Adjust the current cart

Save this basket for next time

Next shop

  1. Saved basket
  2. Continue shopping

Repeat Basket

Start the next shop from a saved basket

Why this sequence

Repeat Basket only becomes useful after the customer has a basket worth returning to.

I would first prove that Basket Optimizer helps customers resolve the current cart, then explore whether the basket they end with is useful enough to carry into future shops.

What this direction depends on

Reliable catalogue data and clear eligibility rules are necessary for the product to surface options customers can trust.

06 — Validation

If customers do not want to change the cart after seeing the total, there is no reason to build Basket Optimizer.

What I need to see

  1. Need

    Do customers want to spend less or get more value after seeing the total?

    If the need is rare, the direction stops here.

  2. Decision

    Can customers review an option and decide without going back to search?

    The decision should stay inside the cart.

  3. Checkout

    After the decision, do customers continue to checkout instead of returning to shopping?

    The flow should remove repeated work, not add another step before purchase.

Supporting signals

  • Value

    Did the change lower the total or improve unit value?

  • Undo

    Are customers repeatedly undoing the same type of change?

Decision gate

ContinueThe need appears, customers can act on it inside the cart, and they continue toward checkout.

Stop or rethinkThe need is weak, or the flow creates more work than it removes.
